Zero doubles is not twelve independent surprises

The bodies contain 1,018 adjacent pairs and no equal neighbours. In a fixed read-then-shuffle key, a particular pair of plaintext letters either always produces a double or never does. Repeated occurrences of that pair are not independent trials.

In a 400-key simulation using English game-text samples, 27 plaintext symbols and three shuffle classes, 4.8% of keys and text samples produced no doubles. That does not establish the probability for the unknown plaintext, but it removes the argument that an ordinary random key would need extraordinary luck. The condition and the experiment

About 183 billion Lua-recipe candidates, no hit

Ten runs covered LuaJIT shuffle recipes and character-table layouts with LuaJIT, MSVC and glibc random generators. The best logged candidate reached 44 of 918 scored cells. This rules out the searched configurations under their test, not seeded shuffles in general. Ranges, scoring and other searches

A guess passing the checks is not a solution

In one exploratory test, 3 of 120 deliberately wrong full-length fills passed the folding closure check when each letter could have its own shuffle. None passed with either of the two tested class splits. Those are rejection rates for constructed fills, not probabilities that a surviving guess is right. A claimed solution needs a key and a reproducible encryption, not just text that fits the repeats. What the checker establishes

The shuffles, alphabet and plaintext remain unknown. The class-shuffle model explains several patterns without a recovered key; it is not the only possible mechanism. Community references and the small reproducible checks are on Sources & tools.
