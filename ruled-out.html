Constraints & searches

An exclusion applies to a specified mechanism and assumptions, not every cipher with a similar name. R and W distinguish repeat-supported plaintext correspondence from whole-passage correspondence. Neither replaces the other model conditions.

Short contradictions

MechanismConstraint
One fixed substitution shared by all messagesEqual letters must give equal symbols. The repeat-supported cells in the E1/E2 example do not. Needs R.
One fixed shuffle after every letterTen steps would take 68 to both 41 and 26. Needs R, fixed read positions and the same initial deck/phase in every message. Certificate.
Copy maps that must be rotations of one fixed ringRotations commute. Two maps from one E3 passage give 54 in one order and 10 in the other. This excludes that representation, including plain cyclic deck walks. Needs W and the rotation property of the proposed mechanism. Worked example.
A repeating substitution key of period 10 or lessOne position in the period shows at least 61 distinct symbols for every period 1–10. A substitution of A plaintext letters has at most A outputs. This excludes those periods for A < 59, the reported bound, if the key restarts with each message. Needs neither R nor W. Count the symbols.
Two published plaintext proposalsThe English and Finnish proposals give E1, W1 and E2 different first lines despite their identical first 24 body symbols. They fail under a shared-start, deterministic, uniquely decodable cipher with the proposed alignment. That conclusion needs this decoding model, not R or W.

Ordering tests have narrower conclusions

No two messages share even their final symbol. Reversing the output therefore does not explain the displayed shared openings as shared plaintext openings under a common-start cipher. It does not rule out encrypting reversed plaintext or using unrelated plaintext endings.

An exhaustive check of non-identity within-block permutations and phases for block sizes 2–8 found none preserving both the five opening-divergence positions and the selected offset-one isomorphs at the tested nearby shifts. This tests a particular preservation requirement, not arbitrary whole-message transposition. Recorded counts.

A statistical objection is not an impossibility

After masking the selected copies, aligned cells in unrelated messages agree 11 times out of 1,100 comparisons. That is low for same-position substitutions of ordinary letter-frequency text. Random homophone selection, on the other hand, must account for the long exactly shared openings. These observations disfavor the tested simple homophonic explanations; neither proves that every homophonic selection rule is impossible without plaintext assumptions.

Searched without a hit

Each key decrypts messages until it encounters a symbol it cannot produce. The score counts those prefixes out of 918 (shared openings counted once); it is not a language score. The Lua protocol required at least 800 and readable text for a hit. No logged candidate approached that threshold. A variant specifies the alphabet layout, letter classes and details such as whether the initial deck or the shuffles were generated first.
